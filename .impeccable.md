# IDM Disclosure Microsite — Design Context

**Project:** Independent policy reference site for the paper *"What Should Frontier AI Developers Disclose About Internal Deployments?"* (arXiv:2604.23065)
**Local:** `/Users/nomads/Nomads/IDM/site/` · **Remote target:** `/nomads/IDM`
**Stack:** Astro (static-first, light interactivity for tracker filters and fillable form)
**Attribution:** Site is paper-attributed only — no Resonant Research or MATS branding. Author names appear in citation footer, not in chrome.

---

## Design Context

### Users
Three distinct readers, all skim-first then deep-read selectively:

1. **AI safety / policy staff at frontier labs** — looking for the disclosure framework as a checklist they can defend internally. They want structure and downloadable artifacts more than prose.
2. **Legislative and regulatory staff** — looking at the tracker to see how their jurisdiction compares, what the gap-to-fix looks like, and to find primary text fast. They cite from this; permalinks and primary-source links matter more than design flourish.
3. **Researchers and journalists** — looking for a citable URL, the framework table, and the reading list. They're checking the work, not browsing.

None of them are here to be entertained. All of them are time-pressured. The site earns trust by being *useful and visibly serious*, not by being clever.

### Brand Personality
- **Three words:** Authoritative · Considered · Alive
- **Voice:** Calm, precise, declarative. Sentences carry weight. No marketing register, no hedging-by-default, no "we believe" hand-raising. Closer to a well-edited policy journal than a research blog.
- **Emotional goal:** A reader should feel they've arrived at the *primary source* on this question — not at someone's project page about it.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Synthesis:** Think-tank substance with editorial-press execution. Stripe Press / Works in Progress typographic discipline applied to policy reference content. Not Brookings-formal, not magazine-flashy, not lab-blog-warm.

**Palette (light theme — primary):**
- Paper: `#FBFAF7` (warm off-white, very slightly cream — avoids clinical pure white)
- Ink: `#16140F` (near-black with warmth)
- Muted ink: `#6B655C` (secondary text, metadata)
- Hairline: `#E4DED2` (rules, borders — very light, never heavy)
- Accent (single, restrained): `#2A4A6B` (oxford blue — policy-credible, not corporate-bright)
- Citation/highlight: `#8A4A1F` (deep amber — used sparingly for grade indicators, primary-source markers)

**Palette (dark theme):**
- Paper: `#14130F` (warm charcoal, not flat black)
- Ink: `#EDE8DD` (warm off-white)
- Muted: `#8A847A`
- Hairline: `#2A2823`
- Accent: `#6B95C2` (lifted oxford blue for contrast)
- Citation/highlight: `#C68B5B`

**Typography:**
- Display / headings: **Source Serif 4** (variable, free, optimized for screen reading) — use weights 400/600. Tight tracking on display sizes.
- Body: **Inter** at 17–18px base, generous line-height (1.55), measure capped at ~68 chars
- Data / legal codes / tracker IDs: **JetBrains Mono** at 13–14px
- Small caps eyebrows for section labels (tracking +0.12em, 12px, muted)

**Layout:**
- Generous whitespace. Margins do work that decoration would otherwise do.
- Single-column reading widths (~640px) for prose; two-column or wide grids only for the tracker and framework tables.
- Hairline rules instead of cards-with-shadows. The whole site should feel like one continuous sheet of paper, not a collection of tiles.
- Sticky section navigation on long pages (framework, tracker) — left rail on desktop, top bar on mobile.

**Motion:**
- Almost none. No hero animations, no scroll-triggered reveals, no count-up numbers.
- What motion exists: 150ms ease on hover/focus states, quiet expand/collapse on framework category drawers, smooth filter transitions on the tracker. That's it.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected throughout.

**Anti-references — what this is NOT:**
- ❌ Generic SaaS landing (hero + gradient CTA + feature cards)
- ❌ Crypto/AI-startup dark mode (black + neon, glitch type, terminal feel)
- ❌ Government-PDF-on-the-web (Times New Roman, blue underlined links, no whitespace)
- ❌ Heavy data-viz dashboard (KPI tiles, animated counters, chart-first hierarchy)

The tracker has metrics but they should read as *editorial summary lines*, not dashboard tiles.

### Design Principles

1. **Substance is the design.** Every visual choice should make the underlying content easier to use, cite, or trust. If a visual element doesn't serve one of those three jobs, cut it.

2. **Earn credibility through restraint.** This site competes with Brookings PDFs and lab system cards for attention from busy regulators. Quiet confidence beats loud personality. One accent color, one display face, one body face — and discipline.

3. **Citation-first.** Every framework row, tracker entry, and checklist item should be linkable, copyable, and quotable. Permalinks, primary-source links, and structured data are not extras — they're the product.

4. **Reading is the primary action.** Optimize type, measure, contrast, and rhythm for reading 1,000+ words at a sitting. The framework page and per-bill assessments are not skim targets — they're documents.

5. **Alive, not flashy.** "Alive" comes from sharp typography, considered hierarchy, and felt-attention to detail (proper kerning on display type, hairlines that align, dark-mode that isn't just inverted) — not from animation or color. A reader should sense that someone *cared*, without being able to point to what made them feel that way.
